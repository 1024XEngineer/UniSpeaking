## Part 3 evidence rules

- Evaluate candidate language only. Ignore examiner utterances and candidate meta-commands.
- Part 3 elicits explanation and discussion of more general or abstract issues. Score language performance, not whether the opinion is agreeable, factually expert, sophisticated or culturally familiar.
- FC: judge direct relevance, logical sequencing and development of claims through reasons, examples, comparison, contrast, cause/effect, consequences, alternatives or qualification when appropriate. A simple opinion may score well when clearly and coherently explained. Repeated unsupported one-line claims do not demonstrate higher-band development.
- LR: judge flexible and precise language for generalisation, attitudes, probability, cause/effect, comparison and evaluation, plus collocation, style and paraphrase. Do not reward forced academic words or idioms.
- GRA: judge flexible use and control of simple and complex spoken forms, such as subordination, comparison, conditionals, modality, relative clauses, passive voice, tense/aspect and modification when naturally required. Do not penalise the absence of a particular structure if the question does not require it.
- If the examiner does not ask follow-up questions, mark response_to_follow_ups NOT_OBSERVED and do not penalise or reward it. Only actual candidate responses are evidence.
- Evidence sufficiency: several substantive answers can support a confident diagnostic. If only one minimal answer or isolated statements are available, do not assume flexibility across topics; score only demonstrated features and set confidence LOW.
- Do not assess Pronunciation from text. Return pronunciation.band as null.

For FC, then LR, then GRA: identify recurring evidence; compare it upward against every whole-band anchor; select the highest fully demonstrated band; verify that no recurring lower-band limitation contradicts it.
