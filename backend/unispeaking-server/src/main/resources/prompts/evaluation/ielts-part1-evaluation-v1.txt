## Part 1 evidence rules

- Evaluate candidate language only. Ignore examiner utterances, the name/introduction exchange, and candidate meta-commands that do not answer a question.
- Part 1 tests communication on familiar, personal topics through several short answers. A relevant, natural 15-30 second answer does not need a formal introduction, conclusion, multiple examples, abstract analysis, or a Part 2-style long turn.
- FC: judge whether answers address the question directly, remain coherent, and extend naturally with details, reasons or examples when appropriate. Across the Part, check continuity, repetition, self-correction, discourse markers and linking. Do not penalise normal concise answers; do penalise a recurring pattern of one-word, fragmentary, irrelevant or undeveloped answers.
- LR: judge variety, precision, appropriacy, collocation and paraphrase across the familiar topics actually asked. Do not award a high band for one memorised idiom or penalise the absence of technical vocabulary.
- GRA: judge range and control across all answers, including accurate basic forms and naturally occurring subordination, tense/aspect, modality and modification. Do not demand a specific advanced structure when the question does not call for it.
- Evidence sufficiency: multiple substantive answers across the Part can support a confident diagnostic. If only an introduction, one minimal answer, isolated words, or meta-commands are present, score only demonstrated features and set confidence LOW. Do not fill missing evidence with assumptions.
- Do not assess Pronunciation from text. Return pronunciation.band as null.

## Decision procedure

For FC, then LR, then GRA: identify recurring evidence; compare it upward against every whole-band anchor; select the highest fully demonstrated band; verify that no recurring lower-band limitation contradicts it. Cite brief exact candidate evidence for every criterion. Keep evidence and comments faithful to the transcript.
